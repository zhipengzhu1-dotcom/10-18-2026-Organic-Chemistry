# Consensus Organic Chemistry I / II sequence across five universities

Research for issue #2 (map #1). Question: which Unit sequence do standard undergraduate Orgo I and Orgo II courses share, where the I/II split falls, which topics vary, which textbooks the courses use, and which openly licensed textbook follows the consensus.

Sources are the links in `Syllabus/Websites.docx`, fetched 2026-10-18. Vocabulary follows `GLOSSARY.md`: Subject › Course › Unit › Lesson › Step. A "Unit" below is roughly one textbook chapter.

## Answer in brief

- **Orgo I (Units 1–8):** structure and bonding → acids and bases → alkanes and conformations → stereochemistry → overview of reactions (mechanism, arrows, thermodynamics, kinetics) → alkenes (electrophilic addition) → alkynes → alkyl halides (SN1/SN2/E1/E2).
- **Orgo II (Units 9–17):** spectroscopy (IR, MS, NMR) → alcohols, ethers and epoxides (with organometallics) → conjugation and Diels–Alder → aromaticity and electrophilic aromatic substitution (EAS) → aldehydes and ketones → carboxylic acids and their derivatives → enols and enolates → amines → biomolecules.
- **The split:** all five schools end Orgo I once substitution/elimination and alkene/alkyne additions are done. Carbonyl chemistry (aldehydes, ketones, acid derivatives, enolates) is the core of Orgo II everywhere. Three topics move between the two terms: aromaticity/EAS, spectroscopy, and alcohols.
- **Recommended open textbook:** *Organic Chemistry* (OpenStax, 2023), John McMurry's 10th edition. It is mirrored on LibreTexts under **CC BY-NC-SA 4.0**, the same licence the site uses for lesson content. Its chapter order is the consensus order. MIT 5.12 used the commercial 6th edition of the same book.

## Per-school evidence

### MIT 5.12 (Orgo I, Spring 2005) and 5.13 (Orgo II, Fall 2006)

- **5.12 textbook:** McMurry, *Organic Chemistry*, 6th ed., Brooks/Cole 2004 ([5.12 readings](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/5-12-organic-chemistry-i-spring-2005/pages/readings/)).
- **5.12 order** ([calendar](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/5-12-organic-chemistry-i-spring-2005/pages/calendar/)): review, resonance, acidity/basicity → alkanes/cycloalkanes, conformational analysis → stereochemistry → overview of reactions, thermodynamics, kinetics, reaction coordinate diagrams → alkenes, HX addition, reactions of alkenes → alkynes → alkyl halides, nucleophilic substitution, elimination → aromaticity → EAS and nucleophilic aromatic substitution → alcohols and phenols → introduction to carbonyls → multistep synthesis. Readings run McMurry Ch 1–11, then 15–17, then 19.
- **5.13 textbook:** McMurry, *Fundamentals of Organic Chemistry*, 6th ed. (2006), required. Wade 5th ed. and Clayden are listed as additional ([5.13 syllabus](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/5-13-organic-chemistry-ii-fall-2006/pages/syllabus/)).
- **5.13 order** ([calendar](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/5-13-organic-chemistry-ii-fall-2006/pages/calendar/)): structure elucidation (elemental analysis, MS, IR, ¹³C and ¹H NMR) → ethers, epoxides, sulfides → conjugated systems and MO theory → pericyclic reactions (electrocyclic, cycloadditions, sigmatropic; Woodward–Hoffmann) → amines → carboxylic acids and derivatives → enols and enolates → carbocations → radicals → photochemistry.
- **Notable:** aromaticity, EAS and the start of carbonyl chemistry sit in Orgo I. Orgo II has an unusually heavy physical-organic and pericyclic load.

### UC Berkeley CHEM 12A / 12B (chemistry-major track)

- **12A, Fall 2020** (Schepartz lecture, Baranger lab) ([TBP syllabus PDF](https://tbp.studentorg.berkeley.edu/syllabi/1310/download/)):
  - **Textbook:** Karty, *Organic Chemistry* 2nd ed. (Norton).
  - **Lecture order (Karty chapters):** 1 atomic and molecular structure → 2 three-dimensional geometry → 3 orbital interactions → 4–5 isomerism → 6 proton transfer (acid–base) → 7 common elementary steps → 8 multistep mechanisms → 9–10 nucleophilic substitution and elimination → 11–12 electrophilic addition → 13 synthesis → 14 conjugation and aromaticity.
  - **Lab:** NMR is taught from the start, together with TLC, extraction, chromatography and HPLC.
- **12A AXS overview (Spring 2018)** ([PDF](https://axs.studentorg.berkeley.edu/classdes/CHEM%2012A.pdf)): orbitals, bonding, structure → conformations, stereochemistry → SN2/SN1/E2/E1 → electrophilic addition to alkenes and alkynes → aromaticity. It describes a HOMO→LUMO, "four elementary steps" approach rather than memorisation.
- **12B AXS overview (Spring 2018)** ([PDF](https://axs.studentorg.berkeley.edu/classdes/CHEM%2012B.pdf)): nucleophilic addition (strong and weak nucleophiles) → enolate chemistry → nucleophilic addition–elimination (acyl substitution) → functional-group interconversion and protecting groups → aromatic substitution (electrophilic and nucleophilic) and heterocycles → cross-coupling (Suzuki, Stille, Sonogashira, Heck, Kumada) → pericyclic reactions → radical reactions.
- **12B catalog entry (Spring 2025)** ([classes.berkeley.edu](https://classes.berkeley.edu/content/2025-spring-chem-12b-001-lec-001)): "nomenclature, chemical and physical properties, reactions and syntheses of the major classes of organic compounds … reaction mechanisms, multistep syntheses, and the chemistry of polycyclic and heterocyclic compounds." It names no textbook and points to the campus store.
- **Notable:** substitution/elimination comes before electrophilic addition. The course is organised by mechanism type and is the only one in the set that teaches cross-coupling.

### University of Michigan CHEM 210 / 215 (Coppola, *Structure and Reactivity*)

Treat them as medium confidence.
- **Textbook:** Coppola, *Structure and Reactivity: An Introduction to Organic Chemistry*, Books A–D (bundle ISBN 978-1-64565-276-2).
- **210 (Ch 1–10):**
  1. Introduction to structure and reactivity
  2. Molecular structure and properties
  3. Acid–base reactions
  4. Physical organic chemistry: energetics and mechanism
  5. Stereoisomerism
  6. Conformational analysis
  7. Substitution and elimination at polar σ bonds
  8. Electrophilic addition I
  9. Electrophilic addition II
  10. Title not retrieved
- **215:**
  - Book C, "Carbonyl Reactions, Transformations, and Synthesis": Ch 11 alcohols and epoxides; Ch 12 aldehydes and ketones; Ch 13 acid derivatives; Ch 14 α-carbon acids (enolates).
  - Book D, "Special Topics": Ch 15 Diels–Alder; Ch 16 carbohydrates; Ch 17 amino acids and proteins.

### UCLA CHEM 30A/B/C (majors, three quarters) and 14C/14D (life-science track)

- **30A, Winter 2005** (Cantrill) ([syllabus PDF](https://www.chem.ucla.edu/~cantrill/30A_W05/30A_W05_Syllabus.pdf)):
  - **Textbook:** Brown & Foote, *Organic Chemistry*, 3rd ed.
  - **Order:** Ch 1 structure and bonding → Ch 2 conformational analysis → Ch 3 stereochemistry → Ch 4 acids and bases → Ch 6 introduction to organic reactions → Ch 5, 6, 10 electrophilic addition to C–C π bonds → Ch 8 nucleophilic substitution, elimination, substitution vs elimination → Ch 7 radical reactions.
- **Current catalog descriptions** (2023 catalog pages, fetched directly):
  - **30A:** bonding, shapes, stereochemistry, acid/base; alkanes, cycloalkanes, alkenes, alkynes; SN2, SN1, elimination, radical reactions.
  - **30B:** alcohols, ethers, sulfur compounds, aldehydes, ketones, carboxylic acids and derivatives; organometallics; MS, IR, ¹H and ¹³C NMR.
  - **30C:** enolates, enamines, dicarbonyls, amines; MO theory and conjugated π systems, UV/vis; aromaticity and aromatic reactions; heterocycles, pericyclic reactions, carbohydrates, lipids.
  - **[14C](https://catalog.registrar.ucla.edu/course/2023/chem14c) "Structure of Organic Molecules":** resonance, stereochemistry, conjugation, aromaticity; NMR, IR, MS; structure–property effects; biomolecular structure.
  - **14D:** nucleophilic and electrophilic substitutions and additions; EAS; carbonyl reactions; catalysis; molecular basis of drug action and pharmaceuticals.
- **Notable:** quarter system. 30A is roughly Orgo I; 30B and 30C together are roughly Orgo II, with aromaticity/EAS last. The 14C/14D track splits by structure vs reactions rather than by functional group.

### UNC CHEM 261 / 262

- **Catalog** ([PDF](https://catalog.unc.edu/courses/chem/chem.pdf)):
  - **261:** "molecular structure and its determination by modern physical methods, correlation between structure and reactivity … classification of reaction types … molecules of biological importance."
  - **262:** "continuation of CHEM 261, with particular emphasis on the chemical properties of organic molecules of biological importance."
- **Beaver's 262 syllabus** (from a study-abroad proposal dated October 2019; its course calendar is an example from an earlier summer session) ([PDF](https://administrativeboards.web.unc.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/7409/2020/04/UNC-Science-in-Germany-Beavers.pdf)):
  - **Textbook:** Bruice, *Organic Chemistry*, 8th ed.
  - **Review list from 261:** bonding and formulas (Ch 1), acid–base (Ch 2), functional groups and nomenclature (Ch 3), stereochemistry (Ch 4), fundamental mechanism processes (Ch 5), nucleophilic substitution (Ch 9).
  - **262 order:** Ch 14 NMR → Ch 8 Diels–Alder, benzene structure, aromaticity, Frost diagrams → Ch 18 EAS (halogenation, nitration, sulfonation, Friedel–Crafts, directing effects) → Ch 11 organometallics → Ch 16 aldehydes and ketones (Grignard, hydrides, acetals, imines and enamines, Wittig, conjugate addition) → Ch 20 carbohydrates → Ch 15 carboxylic acids and derivatives → Ch 17 α-carbon chemistry (aldol, Claisen, Michael, Robinson, malonic ester) → Ch 19 amines (reductive amination) → Ch 21 amino acids.
- **Notable:** NMR, aromaticity and EAS are all Orgo II. Ketones come before acid derivatives, the same order as Berkeley 12B. Biomolecules are interleaved with the functional-group chapters.

## Where the I/II split falls

- **Same at all five schools:** Units 1–8 are Orgo I, and the carbonyl family (Units 13–15) is Orgo II.
- **Variable:** aromaticity/EAS, spectroscopy, and alcohols/ethers move across the boundary.
  - MIT puts aromaticity, EAS and alcohols in Orgo I.
  - Berkeley puts aromaticity in Orgo I and EAS in Orgo II.
  - UCLA and UNC put all three in Orgo II.
- **Recommendation:** make Orgo I = Units 1–8 (OpenStax Ch 1–11) and Orgo II = Units 9–17 (OpenStax Ch 12–26, plus Ch 30 for pericyclic). Every school agrees with this split for the core topics, and it matches the open textbook's chapter boundaries.

## Topics that vary (and where)

- **Substitution/elimination before alkene addition:** Berkeley 12A (2020) and UMich 210. MIT 5.12, UCLA 30A and McMurry/OpenStax put addition first. Both orders are common; OpenStax order is the safer default.
- **Radicals:** in Orgo I at UCLA 30A; in Orgo II at MIT 5.13 and Berkeley 12B.
- **Pericyclic reactions with orbital-symmetry rules:** heavy at MIT 5.13, also in Berkeley 12B and UCLA 30C; optional at UMich (Ch 20). Only Diels–Alder appears at UNC.
- **Transition-metal cross-coupling and protecting groups:** Berkeley 12B only.
- **Carbocation rearrangements and photochemistry as separate Units:** MIT 5.13 only.
- **Biomolecules:** carbohydrates and amino acids at UNC, UMich and UCLA; lipids at UCLA 30C. MIT and Berkeley omit them.
- **Drug action and pharmaceuticals:** UCLA 14D only.
- **Spectroscopy timing:** start of Orgo II at MIT and UNC; in 30B at UCLA; woven into the lab from day one at Berkeley.

## Open textbook

**OpenStax *Organic Chemistry* (McMurry, 10th edition, 2023)** is the best fit.

- **Licence:** the LibreTexts mirror states it is "shared under a CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 license" ([LibreTexts](https://chem.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Organic_Chemistry/Organic_Chemistry_(OpenStax))). That matches the map's lesson-content licence, so adapted material can keep its ShareAlike terms. OpenStax's own pages call it "open" without naming the licence in what was fetched. Rice's announcement confirms McMurry released the 10th edition through OpenStax ([Rice news, 2022](https://news.rice.edu/news/2022/openstax-publish-new-edition-bestselling-organic-chemistry-textbook-and-it-will-be-free)).
- **Chapter order:**
  - Ch 1–6: structure, acids and bases, alkanes, cycloalkanes, stereochemistry, overview of reactions
  - Ch 7–9: alkenes and alkynes
  - Ch 10–11: organohalides, substitution and elimination
  - Ch 12–13: MS, IR, NMR
  - Ch 14: conjugation and UV
  - Ch 15–16: aromaticity and EAS
  - Ch 17–18: alcohols, ethers, epoxides
  - Ch 19: aldehydes and ketones
  - Ch 20–21: acids and derivatives
  - Ch 22–23: α-substitution and condensations
  - Ch 24: amines and heterocycles
  - Ch 25–28: biomolecules
  - Ch 29: metabolic pathways
  - Ch 30: pericyclic reactions
  - Ch 31: polymers
- **Fit:** this is Units 1–17 of the consensus, nearly one-to-one. It is also the commercial lineage MIT 5.12 used.
